# Example: Professional Service Business

Illustrative only. Not a literal template.

**Input context (abbreviated):** Boutique accounting firm for e-commerce businesses, 3 partners, mostly referral-based client acquisition, wants to reduce dependency on referrals and build a predictable pipeline, no real marketing has been done.

---

## Business Diagnosis

The firm has validated demand (referrals keep coming) but has no owned acquisition channel, which means growth is capped by the partners' existing network rather than market size. The core marketing problem is building a first channel from zero, not optimizing an existing one.

## Marketing Opportunity Radar

Biggest Growth Opportunity: narrow, visible specialization in e-commerce accounting specifically (inventory accounting, multi-channel sales tax, marketplace fee reconciliation) as a wedge, since "accounting firm" alone is an undifferentiated, low-intent search category.

Biggest Marketing Weakness: no digital presence that reflects the actual specialization exists yet, so even referred prospects researching the firm find generic or thin information.

Quickest Win: a single, specific page addressing the most common pain point (likely e-commerce sales tax complexity across states or platforms) that referred prospects can be pointed to, improving close rate on warm referrals that already exist.

Biggest Competitive Advantage: genuine category specialization in a niche most general accounting firms don't understand well, which is a strong wedge for long-sales-cycle B2B content and search.

Highest Priority Action: build the specialization-focused web presence first, since it compounds for both existing referral traffic and any future inbound effort, and requires no new channel to start paying off.

## Recommended Strategy

1. Build a focused site or page set around e-commerce accounting specialization, not generic accounting services, to serve both referred prospects and future search traffic.
2. Start light-touch content (3-4 pieces) addressing the specific, recurring questions e-commerce founders ask about accounting, as a foundation for SEO and referral credibility.
3. Formalize the referral motion itself (a simple ask process, partner outreach) as a near-term complement, since it's the proven channel and shouldn't be abandoned while a new one is built.

## Action Plan

First: rebuild or create web presence around the e-commerce specialization within the next month.
Second: publish the first 2-3 specific content pieces addressing real, recurring client questions.
Third: revisit paid or outbound channels only after the owned content foundation exists, since cold traffic needs somewhere credible to land.

## Success Metrics

Inbound inquiries from non-referral sources, referral-to-client close rate (should improve once prospects have something credible to review), organic search visibility for e-commerce-accounting-specific terms.

## Final Verdict

Don't chase a new acquisition channel yet. Build the specialized web presence first so referrals convert better and any future channel has somewhere credible to send traffic.
