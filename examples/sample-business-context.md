# BUSINESS_CONTEXT.md (filled example)

Illustrative only, shows what a reasonably complete filled-in context document looks like. Fictional business.

## 1. Business Name and Overview

Driftwell. A sleep tracking app for people with irregular schedules (shift workers, new parents, frequent travelers) who don't fit standard circadian-rhythm sleep advice.

## 2. Business Background

Started 18 months ago by a solo founder who was a nurse working rotating shifts and found existing sleep apps useless for anyone not on a normal schedule.

## 3. Product or Service Details

Mobile app. Tracks sleep via phone sensors, gives personalized recommendations for irregular schedules specifically, adaptive rather than fixed-bedtime advice.

## 4. Business Model

Freemium. Free tier tracks basic sleep data. $7.99/month premium unlocks personalized recommendations and schedule planning.

## 5. Revenue Streams

Subscription only, no ads, no data sales.

## 6. Business Stage

Early stage, post-launch. 22,000 free users, 900 paying subscribers, about 14 months since premium tier launched.

## 7. Business Objectives

Grow premium conversion rate (currently roughly 4%) and reduce premium churn (currently unknown, not tracked well).

## 8. Target Market

English-speaking markets initially (US, UK, Canada, Australia). No specific geographic focus beyond that.

## 9. Customer Segments

Three visible segments in the data: shift workers (nurses, factory, security), new parents, frequent business travelers. Shift workers are the largest and most engaged segment so far.

## 10. Ideal Customer Profile

Shift worker, usually healthcare or emergency services, has tried generic sleep apps and found the advice useless or actively unhelpful for a rotating schedule.

## 11. Customer Problems and Desired Outcomes

Problem: standard sleep advice assumes a fixed schedule and doesn't help. Desired outcome: feel less exhausted and foggy despite an irregular schedule, without needing to quit the job causing it.

## 12. Value Proposition

The only sleep app built for irregular schedules instead of pretending everyone sleeps 11pm to 7am.

## 13. Differentiation

Adaptive scheduling logic is genuinely different from competitors, who mostly use the same fixed-circadian-rhythm framework regardless of marketing claims.

## 14. Pricing and Packages

Free tier (basic tracking), Premium $7.99/month or $59/year.

## 15. Competitors

Sleep Cycle, Pillow, generic big-name sleep trackers built into phones. None specifically target irregular schedules.

## 16. Existing Alternatives

Doing nothing and just being tired. Generic sleep apps used incorrectly. Caffeine as a coping mechanism (anecdotal, mentioned by users in reviews).

## 17. Current Marketing Channels

App Store search (unoptimized), a small amount of Instagram content, occasional mentions in shift-worker and nursing online communities, no paid spend.

## 18. Sales Process

Self-serve, download and freemium upgrade, no sales team.

## 19. Customer Journey

Mostly App Store search or community mention, download free tier, use for a few weeks, convert to premium if the free tier's limitations become frustrating.

## 20. Existing Marketing Performance

4% free-to-paid conversion rate. App Store conversion rate and churn not currently tracked well.

## 21. Available Customer Research

App Store reviews (mostly positive from shift workers specifically, mixed from people trying to use it for normal fixed schedules). No formal interviews conducted yet.

## 22. Brand Voice

Direct, a little irreverent, not clinical. Founder wants it to feel like it was built by someone who's actually lived the problem, not a wellness brand.

## 23. Budget and Resources

Solo founder, roughly $500/month available for marketing, no team.

## 24. Operational Constraints

Founder still works part-time shifts, limited hours available for marketing work.

## 25. Previous Marketing Experiments

Tried general sleep-health content on Instagram early on, low engagement. Posts specifically about shift work got noticeably more engagement and saves.

## 26. Current Challenges

Conversion rate feels low, unsure if it's a pricing issue, an onboarding issue, or a targeting issue (too many non-irregular-schedule users in the free tier diluting the data).

## 27. Assumptions and Unknowns

Unsure whether shift workers are actually the best segment to focus all marketing on versus staying broad across all three segments. No churn data for premium subscribers yet.
