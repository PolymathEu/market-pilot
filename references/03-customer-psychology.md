# Module 3: Customer Psychology and Research

## What to understand

- Ideal customer profile and customer segments (there's often more than one; don't force a single ICP if the context shows otherwise)
- Jobs to be done: what the customer is actually trying to accomplish, independent of the product
- Pain points, framed as specifically as the context allows
- Desired outcomes, in the customer's language rather than the company's
- Buying triggers: what event or realization makes someone start looking for a solution now
- Emotional motivations underneath the rational ones (status, fear, relief, ambition, belonging)
- Purchase objections and perceived risks
- Decision-making criteria and who's actually involved in the decision (especially relevant for B2B)
- Trust requirements: what has to be true before this customer will buy
- Alternatives the customer is actually weighing, including inertia

## Jobs to Be Done, applied properly

Don't describe the customer demographically when the "job" is what matters. "35-44 year old marketing manager" says little about why they'd buy. "Needs to prove campaign ROI to a skeptical CFO before the next budget cycle" says a lot. Where context supports it, frame segments around the job and the trigger, not just the title or demographic.

## Don't invent research

If the Project includes actual customer interviews, reviews, survey data, or support tickets, use them and quote patterns you actually see (without exact long quotes, per copyright norms). If no customer research exists in context, say so and work from the user's own stated understanding of their customers, clearly labeled as a claim rather than verified research. Never generate quotes, testimonials, or specific customer statements that don't exist in the source material.

## Ethical persuasion only

Behavioral economics and persuasion principles (scarcity, social proof, loss aversion, anchoring, reciprocity) are fair game, but only where they reflect something true. Manufactured urgency, fake scarcity, or exploiting a vulnerability that isn't genuinely tied to the product's value are off the table. If a persuasion tactic only works by misleading the customer, don't recommend it.

## Output for this module

Feeds Positioning, Offer, and Campaign modules. In the final brief this rarely gets its own section unless specifically requested; it shows up as the "why" behind positioning and messaging recommendations.
