# Module 1: Business Diagnosis

The purpose of this module is to find what is actually preventing the business from growing right now. Everything downstream depends on getting this right; a brilliant campaign strategy is wasted if the real problem is product market fit.

## What to analyze

- Business model and revenue model (how money actually gets made, not just what's sold)
- Product or service and its core value proposition
- Growth stage (pre-PMF, post-PMF scaling, mature, declining)
- Commercial viability signals (unit economics if available, growth rate, retention)
- Strengths and weaknesses, stated plainly
- Operational constraints (team size, budget, technical capability, time)
- Marketing bottlenecks specifically (as distinct from product or operational bottlenecks)
- Product market fit signals: organic growth, retention curves, word of mouth, willingness to pay, usage intensity
- Root causes, not symptoms

## Finding the real bottleneck

Most businesses that ask for "marketing help" actually have one of a few underlying problems, and the marketing strategy should differ substantially depending on which one it is:

1. **No product market fit yet.** Marketing can't fix this. If retention is weak or nobody who tries the product sticks around, say so directly. More top-of-funnel spend on a leaky bucket is a waste. The recommendation here should prioritize learning over scaling.
2. **PMF exists but is invisible.** The product works for its users but positioning, messaging, or channel choice fails to communicate it. This is a genuine marketing problem and the one these modules are built for.
3. **PMF exists and marketing works, but it doesn't scale.** Channel saturation, rising CAC, or a single founder-led channel that can't be systematized. The strategy here is about diversification and process, not creative.
4. **Marketing works but the offer or pricing leaves value on the table.** Growth is fine but margins or expansion revenue are weak.

State which of these (or combination) seems to be happening, and why, using the evidence available. If the evidence doesn't clearly point to one, say that too rather than forcing a verdict.

## Root cause, not surface symptom

If the user says "we need more leads," don't take the request at face value. Ask (internally, then reflect in the diagnosis): more leads of what quality, converting at what rate, from what channel, compared to what cost. "More leads" is almost never actually the bottleneck; something upstream usually is (unclear offer, wrong channel, weak conversion).

## Output for this module

In the final brief this becomes 2-4 sentences: what stage the business is at, what's actually limiting growth, and why that's the right read of the evidence. Save the detailed reasoning for yourself; the user needs the conclusion and enough rationale to trust it, not the full analysis trail.
