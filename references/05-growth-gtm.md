# Module 5: Growth Strategy and Go-to-Market

## What to develop

- Acquisition strategy and channel selection
- Distribution strategy (how the product reaches the customer, not just how they hear about it)
- Go-to-market plan for a launch or expansion
- Growth loops (where usage or acquisition creates more usage or acquisition, as opposed to a one-off campaign)
- Referral mechanisms, when there's a genuine reason customers would refer
- Partnership opportunities
- Market entry sequencing (which segment or geography first, and why)

## Channel prioritization

Prioritize channels on four things together, not any one alone:

1. **Where the customer already is** (from Module 3's understanding of the customer's behavior)
2. **Business economics** (can this channel plausibly support the business's margins and sales cycle)
3. **Execution capability** (does the team have the skill or budget to actually run this channel well)
4. **Time to signal** (how long before you'd know if it's working)

A channel that's theoretically ideal but the team can't execute (e.g., recommending a content strategy to a team with no writing capacity and no budget to hire) is a bad recommendation even if the channel logic is sound. Recommend 1-3 channels to focus on, not a scattershot list of everything that could theoretically work.

## Business-model-specific channel logic

- **B2B, longer sales cycle**: often outbound, content/SEO for inbound, partnerships, events, founder-led sales early on
- **B2C, low price point**: often paid social, organic social, influencer/creator, SEO for high-intent terms
- **Marketplace**: chicken-and-egg problem matters more than channel choice; often needs a seeded-supply or seeded-demand strategy before broad acquisition
- **Local business**: local SEO, Google Business Profile, local partnerships, community presence over broad digital spend
- **Early-stage with no customers yet**: manual, unscalable acquisition first (direct outreach, communities) to learn before investing in scalable channels

## Growth loops vs. campaigns

A campaign has a start and end. A loop compounds: more users leads to more distribution leads to more users, without new spend each cycle (referral programs, user-generated content, network effects, SEO content that keeps ranking). Where a genuine loop is plausible for this business, flag it, since it's usually higher leverage than another one-off campaign. Don't force a loop narrative onto a business where none genuinely exists.

## Output for this module

Usually the core of "Recommended Strategy" and "Action Plan" in the comprehensive brief when the request is acquisition-focused.
