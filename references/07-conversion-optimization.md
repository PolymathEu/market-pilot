# Module 7: Conversion Rate Optimization

## What to analyze

- Customer journey from first touch to conversion
- Marketing funnel stage by stage, identifying where drop-off likely concentrates
- Landing page clarity and structure
- Conversion friction: anything that adds effort, confusion, or hesitation
- Trust signals (or their absence): social proof, credentials, guarantees, transparency
- Offer clarity: can a new visitor understand what's being offered and why it matters in seconds
- Purchase objections, mapped back to Module 3
- Checkout or signup experience specifically
- Lead generation process, if lead-gen rather than direct purchase

## Hypothesis-driven recommendations

Every CRO recommendation should take this shape: "Because [evidence or reasonable inference], we believe [specific friction point] is costing conversions. Test: [specific change]. Measure: [specific metric]." Vague advice like "improve your landing page" is not useful; "the hero section leads with a feature list instead of the core outcome, which likely costs clarity in the first five seconds, test leading with the primary outcome statement instead" is useful.

## Where there's no analytics data

If the Project doesn't include funnel or analytics data, don't invent drop-off percentages. Reason qualitatively from the page or flow itself (if the user has shared it) and from what's known about the customer and their objections. Flag clearly that quantitative validation would need real funnel data.

## Common friction patterns worth checking

- Too many decisions required before the user sees value (excessive form fields, forced account creation before trial)
- Value proposition not restated near the conversion point (user forgets why they're here by the time they reach checkout)
- Missing or weak trust signals at the exact moment of hesitation
- Price or commitment revealed too late, creating a sense of being misled
- No clear single next action (multiple competing CTAs)

## Output for this module

When requested specifically, prioritize 2-3 highest-leverage friction points over a long audit list. Each with the hypothesis, the fix, and how to measure it.
