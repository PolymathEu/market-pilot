# Module 6: Campaign and Creative Strategy

## What to develop

- Campaign concepts tied to a specific objective (awareness, consideration, conversion, retention)
- Creative direction and tone, consistent with brand voice if the Project specifies one
- Messaging angles, usually 2-4 distinct ones worth testing rather than one guess
- Content pillars (for an ongoing content strategy, not a single campaign)
- Advertising hooks: the first line or image that earns attention
- Campaign structure (sequencing, channels, timing)
- Landing page messaging that matches the ad or campaign promise (message match matters more than most people think)
- Calls to action, specific to the objective
- Creative testing approach
- Brand communication strategy consistency

## Every creative idea needs a reason

Each recommended angle or hook must trace back to something from Module 3 (a specific pain point, objection, or trigger) or Module 1 (a specific business objective). "This would be a fun campaign" is not sufficient justification. If you can't connect a creative idea to an insight, cut it or flag it as pure speculation.

## Message match discipline

The promise in the ad, email, or hook has to match what the landing page or next step actually delivers. A common, costly failure: clever ad hook, generic landing page that doesn't continue the thought. When reviewing or recommending campaigns, check this explicitly.

## Format and channel fit

Don't recommend a format divorced from the channel's native behavior (e.g., a long-form sales letter style hook for a platform where people scroll past anything that doesn't hook in the first line, or a highly produced video for a channel where raw/authentic performs better). Base this on what's known about the channel and the audience from Module 3, not generic "best practices" lists.

## Output for this module

When requested specifically, give 2-4 distinct creative angles, each with: the insight it's built on, the hook or core message, and which channel/format it fits. Don't write full ad copy unless asked; give direction sharp enough that someone else could write the copy from it.
