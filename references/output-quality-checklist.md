# Output Quality Checklist

Check the final output against this before sending it.

## Writing style

- Reads like a sharp, experienced human consultant wrote it, not like generic AI marketing copy.
- No robotic phrasing, no repetitive sentence openers ("Additionally," "Furthermore," "It's important to note" repeated across sections).
- No exaggerated enthusiasm, no hype language ("game-changing," "revolutionary," "supercharge"), no artificial positivity about a weak strategy.
- No generic marketing jargon dressed up as insight ("leverage synergies," "unlock growth").
- Specific to this business. If you could paste the same paragraph into a brief for a different company with only the name changed, it's too generic, go back and sharpen it.

## No dash-heavy formatting

This is a specific, checked requirement, not a loose stylistic preference.

- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in the visible output.
- Don't default to hyphen-bullet lists for prose-like content. Prefer short paragraphs, numbered lists, or compact tables.
- Where a list is genuinely the right format (e.g., the Opportunity Radar's five items), use it, but keep each line a complete, natural sentence or fragment, not fragments stitched together with dashes.
- Use commas, periods, parentheses, or colons where a dash would otherwise be reached for.

## Structure

- Matches the comprehensive output format from SKILL.md for comprehensive requests, or is appropriately scoped and shorter for narrow requests.
- Headers are short and functional, not decorative.
- Tables used only where they genuinely organize comparable data better than prose.
- Bold used sparingly, only on genuine conclusions or the single most important phrase in a section, not scattered across every paragraph.

## Length discipline

- Comprehensive brief: roughly 300 to 700 words. If it's running long, cut recommendations down to the highest-leverage ones rather than keeping everything at lower confidence.
- Narrow requests: as short as the question allows. Don't pad a specific question with unrequested comprehensive-brief sections.
- No restating the user's question back to them, no unnecessary preamble, no "here's your strategy" framing before diving in.

## Factual integrity

- No invented statistics, market sizes, competitor facts, or customer quotes.
- Assumptions and hypotheses are labeled as such somewhere in the output when they materially affect a recommendation, not presented with the same confidence as known facts.
- No guaranteed outcomes or unsupported revenue/growth projections.
- If a document was referenced but inaccessible, that's noted rather than silently worked around.

## Directness

- Weaknesses are named plainly, not softened into mush. "Positioning doesn't differentiate from the three closest competitors" beats "there may be room to explore differentiation."
- Critique targets the strategy or business model, never the person, and never in a way that reads as a personal judgment.
